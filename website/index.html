02 · THE CASE

From marketplace activity to an executive decision system.

The project is not a collection of isolated charts. It is an analytical operating layer: define trustworthy grains, reconcile the commercial scorecard, distinguish acquisition from seller value, measure activation under censoring, surface concentration, and connect delivery performance to customer experience without overstating causality.

The problem

Marketplace growth can look healthy in aggregate while hiding seller concentration, low-quality acquisition, slow activation, or customer-experience deterioration. A decision-maker needs one view that shows scale and risk together.

The analytical response

Build a consistent semantic layer across marketplace, seller, funnel, retention, and experience domains — then expose the most decision-relevant evidence in a dashboard that always retains the underlying denominator and caveat.

03 · BUSINESS QUESTIONS

Four questions the executive page is designed to answer quickly.

01

Is marketplace momentum real?

Separate volume, GMV proxy, seller count, and AOV so a change in one component is not mistaken for broad-based growth.

02

How concentrated is seller value?

Quantify whether commercial performance depends on a narrow seller cohort rather than a diversified seller base.

03

Where is the commercial contribution coming from?

Expose category and geographic contribution without confusing sales value with platform revenue or profitability.

04

Is customer experience becoming an operating risk?

Track late delivery and review outcomes as diagnostic signals while keeping causal claims out of the dashboard.

04 · TRUST LAYER

Before visualizing anything, define what each number is allowed to mean.

The core design principle is metric governance: grain, denominator, reporting boundary, and inference limit are part of the metric — not footnotes added later.

GMV

GMV proxy

Sum of order_item.price. It represents merchandise-sales value in the historical dataset, not Olist revenue, profit, or margin.

#

Orders

Distinct order_id. Counts are protected from item/payment fan-out instead of counting rows after one-to-many joins.

S

Active sellers

Distinct sellers with at least one item in the period. The operating grain is explicitly seller-period, not raw transaction row.

⌁

Late delivery

Delivered after the estimated date divided by delivered orders. It is an order-level signal and is not assigned as direct seller causality.

REPORTING BOUNDARY CONTRACT

Incomplete edge periods are not allowed to drive executive trend claims.

A month is treated as executive-complete only when it is not an edge month and passes the configured activity/completeness gate. Raw observation, analytical eligibility, and executive reporting boundaries are kept separate.

05 · KEY FINDINGS

What the evidence says — and what it does not.

01
SELLER CONCENTRATION

Marketplace scale is broad, but value creation is highly concentrated.

The top 20% of sellers account for 82.7% of GMV proxy. This makes concentration a commercial resilience issue: seller count alone overstates how diversified marketplace value actually is.

Decision implicationTrack seller concentration alongside GMV growth and maintain a diversification watchlist for high-value seller dependence.
82.7%GMV proxy from top 20% of sellersHistorical portfolio concentration
02
CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

Late delivery is strongly associated with worse review outcomes.

On-time orders average about 4.29 review points versus 2.57 for late orders. The observed mean review gap is approximately −1.73 points; low-review incidence is also substantially higher among late orders.

Decision implicationPrioritize delivery-risk diagnostics by volume, delay severity, geography, category, and carrier before assigning operational ownership.
−1.73Review-point gapLate minus on-time; association only
03
SELLER ACTIVATION

Activation is a time-to-event problem, not a simple matched/unmatched ratio.

Among sellers observable through each fixed window, activation reaches 15.8% by 30 days, 30.8% by 60 days, and 42.1% by 90 days. The median exact time among the 380 observed activators is 44.3 days.

Decision implicationInstrument onboarding around 30/60/90-day milestones and treat sellers without an observed event as censored where appropriate rather than silently dropping them.

06 · DECISION LAYER

The analysis ends with actions, not charts.

01

Protect the operating scorecard

Use grain-safe monthly GMV proxy, orders, active sellers, and AOV; reconcile every refresh and block partial periods from headline trend interpretation.

Data governance
02

Evaluate acquisition on quality, not lead volume

Read origin performance across conversion, matched sellers, activation, and downstream GMV together. CAC and ROAS remain unavailable until spend is added.

Growth
03

Instrument seller onboarding

Monitor first-sale milestones at 7/30/60/90 days and preserve the eligible/censored population logic when comparing channels or segments.

Activation
04

Monitor concentration explicitly

Pair marketplace growth with seller-dependence indicators so strong GMV does not hide fragility in the seller portfolio.

Commercial risk
05

Prioritize delivery diagnostics

Use order volume, delay severity, and review outcomes to prioritize investigation. Do not assign a causal seller fault where multi-seller orders make attribution ambiguous.

WHY FAN-OUT MATTERS

One wrong join can make a good-looking dashboard financially wrong.

The project explicitly demonstrates the item × payment fan-out risk before metric calculation. Commercial values are calculated at documented grains and reconciled back to canonical outputs.

WHY CENSORING MATTERS

Recent sellers have not had equal time to activate or retain.

Activation and retention denominators only include sellers observable through the relevant time window. This prevents newer cohorts from being penalized simply because their future has not happened yet.

08 · LIMITATIONS & INFERENCE BOUNDARY

Credibility comes from showing where the analysis stops.

Historical data

The dataset is anonymized and historical. The project does not claim to describe the current Brazilian e-commerce market.

GMV ≠ revenue

GMV proxy is merchandise value from item prices. Platform revenue, margin, CAC, and ROAS are unavailable.

Sampled funnel

The marketing funnel is sampled and not every lead links to an observed marketplace seller.

Association ≠ causation

Late delivery and review outcomes are observational associations, not experimental causal estimates.

Attribution limits

Multi-seller orders make seller-level delivery/review attribution ambiguous.

Right censoring

Recent cohorts do not have complete future observation windows; ineligible retention cells remain null rather than being forced to zero.
